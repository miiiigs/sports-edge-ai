[6:35 AM, 8/14/2026] Cherona Chadwick: The unsexy truth: it’s the same stack as a quant hedge fund, and most of it is free.

The core language — Python. Pandas/NumPy for data wrangling, scikit-learn and statsmodels for modeling, XGBoost/LightGBM for the machine-learning layer. This is the industry default now. Older shops (Smartodds era) built heavily in R — it was the statistician’s language when Dixon-Coles-style models were being written, and plenty of that code still runs in production.

Where speed matters — C++ or Java. In-play pricing engines and anything latency-sensitive (repricing before the bet delay expires, live steam chasing) gets rewritten out of Python into compiled code. Some newer shops use Rust or Go for this layer.

Databases:

	•	PostgreSQL for the main store — fixtures, results, bets, graded CLV
	•	Time-series databases for odds ticks — kdb+ is the finance-world classic (expensive), TimescaleDB or ClickHouse are the modern free equivalents. Odds snapshots every few seconds across thousands of markets is genuinely a time-series problem.
	•	Redis for live state — current prices in memory for instant lookup

And the honest bit you need before spending a dollar: most people who build this find out in the paper-trading month that their CLV is flat, and the right move is to stop. That’s not failure — that’s the system doing its job of protecting your bankroll. The edge either shows up in the graded numbers or it doesn’t exist. No conviction, no vibes, no “this feels like a winner.”
